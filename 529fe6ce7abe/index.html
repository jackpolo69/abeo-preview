02 · What StarHub is paid, and why it is not a share of your revenue

Every venture running on the mythOS substrate contributes a defined participation to the ecosystem that maintains it. The rate is derived rather than chosen — see section 03. What matters here is where it is taken from.

◆ It is taken from margin, never from top-line revenue

Your rate card runs $36–$44 an hour and your gross margin is $5–$10 of that. A share of top-line revenue would take more than the business makes — on a 30% top-line rate, $10.80–$13.20 an hour against a $5–$10 margin. That is not a hard bargain, it is an impossible one, and the framework explicitly forbids it. Care is a thin-margin business and the instrument has to respect that or it destroys what it is paid from.

The two instruments — one claim, delivered twice

Instrument Rate What it means in practice
Share of net revenue ~17% of net Net = after direct service costs. On a client at $5,000–$10,000 of annual gross margin, that is $850–$1,700 a year. It scales with what the business actually keeps, so it can never outrun your ability to pay it.
Equity ~20%, vesting over 3 years Post-cost by nature — it shares the downside and never pushes the company underwater. Vesting means it is earned across the three years, not granted on day one. ⚠️ See section 05: the destination of this equity is not settled.
The two together One dollar claim The percentages are NEVER added — 20% + 17% is not 37%. Ownership and revenue have different bases and adding them is meaningless. They are two instruments delivering one claim on value created.
Negotiable Yes, and that is written down You may trade a higher revenue share for less equity, or the reverse, with counsel. The framework is the shape, not the verdict.

03 · Where the number comes from

The ecosystem's total participation is 1/e — about 36.8% — of the value a venture creates using the substrate. The venture keeps the remaining 63.2%. Written plainly, because a number nobody can explain is a number nobody should sign:

◆ It is a harvest rate

Take too much from the ventures and they starve — and a commons with no living ventures has nothing to draw from. Take too little and the commons starves — it can no longer maintain the substrate the ventures depend on. Both ends collapse the system. In between there is a sustainable peak, and modelled across decades it lands at roughly 37–39%. 1/e sits in the middle of that band. It is the same logic as a fishing quota: the rate that can be taken indefinitely without killing what produces it.

HONEST PRECISION: the model confirms the structure and that the optimum is APPROXIMATELY 1/e. The exact peak moves with the parameters. So 1/e is a derived value with declared precision — the elegant constant at the centre of a sustainable band — not a claim that the mathematics forces 0.3679 exactly.

StarHub is not a vendor that delivered a website. It is operating the company's commercial layer, and this document should say so.

◆ The setup fee, and how it is handled here

StarHub's standard build carries a setup fee — $50,000 at the Launch tier. For Abeó it is carried, and recovered only from outside capital: an investment, a note, or a grant. It never comes out of operations, and it is never taken from money the business needs to run. If Abeó never raises, it is never recovered. Stating the value rather than waiving it silently is what lets anyone confirm the work was priced on its merits, which matters precisely because the parties are close.

05 · The two open items, and how each is handled

Two items are not finished. Both have a stated path rather than being left to be discovered later.

◆ 1 — The equity issues now, and is assigned onward later

StarHub's long-term intent is that this equity is held by a steward-ownership commons trust rather than on StarHub Studios' own cap table. That trust instrument has not been drafted yet. Rather than leave the clause unexecutable until it exists, the equity issues provisionally to StarHub Studios PBC at signature, subject to a written obligation to assign it to the trust once the trust is formed, on the same terms and with no change to your position either way.

Why it is written this way and not held open: an investor asking who holds what needs a name. "Being restructured" is not an answer anyone can diligence, and an unexecuted ownership clause is the kind of gap that stalls a raise. An assignment obligation is ordinary, familiar to any investor’s counsel, and costs you nothing — the economics above do not move whether the holder is StarHub or the trust.

COUNSEL WORDS THIS, NOT US. The mechanism is routine — provisional issuance with a covenant to assign — but the drafting is a lawyer's job, and the trust's own formation documents will need to accept the assignment. Named here as the agreed intent so both sides are working from the same plan when counsel picks it up.

◆ 2 — This is heads of terms, not the agreement

No equity issuance, no IP transfer, and no capital movement should occur before both parties' counsel have finalised the definitive documents. Nothing in this document is legal or tax advice.

ALSO OPEN, AND SMALLER: the exact split between the equity and net-revenue instruments is negotiable within the one-claim constraint; and the convertible note (the Stage 1 ecosystem loan) sits in a separate instrument and is not restated here.
